How HyperSignal works

Each tab above is a saved monitor — it finds matching roles and lists them below. Nothing leaves your machine unless you add a webhook.

  • Fetch New Jobs pulls fresh postings now for the selected tab. It searches across all results, so it can take a few minutes. A 5-minute cooldown prevents over-running — you can override with a warning.
  • Posted shows the age when it's available; some listings don't include one, shown as “—”. Use Posted within to filter by freshness.
  • Editing a monitor and saving re-checks stored jobs against the new rules. Deleting one removes it and its matches; jobs other monitors still match are kept.
  • Webhook (set per monitor) POSTs new matches to your URL in batches — never old/historical jobs, and never duplicates.
  • Settings lets you auto-hide or delete jobs once they're old, and cap webhook batch size.

Guide

What every part of HyperSignal does, and what every signal means — in plain English.

HyperSignal watches companies for buying signals. It finds who's hiring (from public job boards) and layers on other signs a company is in motion — funding, tool changes, layoffs, launches — all from free public sources, so you reach out at the right moment.

The surfaces
Client Monitoring

Keyword-first. You describe roles (e.g. "customer support") and HyperSignal finds everyone hiring for them, anywhere. Use it to discover new companies you didn't know about.

Account Monitoring

Domain-first. You give a list of companies you already care about and HyperSignal watches them continuously — their hiring plus any signals you enable. Use it for named target accounts.

Account Checking

A one-off snapshot. Upload a CSV of domains, pick the signals to run, and get an instant "who's hiring / what's their current state" grid. Nothing repeats — it runs once and is saved for reference.

Signals

The sales-signal feed beyond hiring. Feed shows changes over time (a company just raised, switched tools, launched). Checks runs a chosen set of signals on an uploaded list on demand.

Jobs database

Every raw job record HyperSignal has stored, across all monitors and checks. Search, filter, edit, or bulk-delete directly — the source data behind everything else.

Technologies

Which tools each company's job posts mention — detected from the title and description of every matched posting. Browse the leaderboard, then drill into the companies using any given technology. This is not a read of their website: what a company's site actually runs is the separate Website tech signal, on the Signals page.

Signals explained
A signal is a sign a company is in motion. Two ways they show up: as a current-state flag ("do they run ads right now?" — used to filter jobs & companies) and as a feed event ("they just started running ads" — a dated change vs the last scan). On a company's first scan, the 10 diff-based signals record a baseline and emit nothing, so you don't get a flood of old news. Four do fire immediately, by design, because each of their findings is a dated event rather than a change: hiring velocity, WARN layoff notices (which reach back 200 days), Product Hunt launches, and "app gone stale" — that last one is measured from the app's own current state, not from a diff. Every signal below is 100% free and public.
Fetch schedules
Every monitor decides when it auto-fetches. Three modes:
  • Every… — a simple interval: every 90 minutes, every 3 days, whatever number you type.
  • At set times — specific time(s) of day on chosen days: 9:00 & 17:00 on weekdays, the 1st of each month, etc.
  • Once — a single run at an exact date & time, then it's done.
The builder shows a live "Next run" so you always know when it'll fire next.
Webhooks & delivery
A monitor can POST new matches and signals to a URL you set (Zapier, Make, your own endpoint). Realtime sends each batch as it's found; scheduled batches them into a digest. You can also route specific signal families to different webhooks. Only new events are sent — never old jobs, never duplicates.

Webhook delivery
New matches POST to a search's webhook in batches of this size, so a big backlog never lands as one giant request. 0 = no limit (everything in one POST). Each request carries X-HyperSignal-Event, -Client, -Batch (e.g. 2/5) and -Count headers, plus a per-job first_seen_at timestamp (reliable even when posted_at is missing). A failed batch stops delivery and retries next cycle — so no duplicates.
